**Subject:** CSWC 60 item 3 — "Mr. Kolbe" at Philippolis, 1834: a correction, and a request for a copy

Dear Centre for Research Collections,

I write about **CSWC 60**, the five photograph copies of sketches by Charles Bell of African
congregations, held in CSWC Small Collections Box 1.

**First, a correction you may want for the catalogue.** Your description of item 3 reads
"Mr. Kolbe, a moravian missionary?, at Philippolis in 1834". The question mark is well
placed: he was not Moravian. He was **London Missionary Society**, and I can give you three
independent sources for it. **I have put the one you can check fastest first.**

- **The London Missionary Society printed him as its own missionary, in its own magazine, the
  year before your sketch was drawn.** *The Evangelical Magazine and Missionary Chronicle*,
  1833, pp. 410–412 carries his letter from Philippolis over the signature **"G. A. KOLBE"**,
  followed by "Extracts from the Journal of Mr. George Augustus Kolbe". The volume is out of
  copyright and freely readable at
  `https://archive.org/details/evangelicalmagaz0011unse` — **the relevant pages are 410 to 412**,
  and you need take nothing in this email on trust.
- The National Library of South Africa's catalogue of **MSB 751, the G.A. Kolbe Collection**,
  describes him as "George Augustus Kolbe, 1802–44. LMS missionary".
- **His father's letter of 23 August 1830**, in that same collection, which I have read: it
  reports that the writer had been told his son was "fully accepted by the [L.M.S.] as one of
  their Missionaries".

For completeness, the fuller shape of his service: **in the field among the San at Boesmanskool
(the present Bethulie) from April 1828**, by his own account in a letter printed in the same
magazine for 1831, and **formally accepted by the Society in February 1829**, which is the date
Sibree's *Register of Missionaries* gives; transferred to **Philippolis in March 1831**;
**ordained on 25 May 1833** by the French missionaries Arbousset and Casalis; and dismissed by
the Society on 19 June 1837. (A draft Dictionary of South African Biography entry held with
MSB 751 gives the acceptance as 8 February 1828, a year earlier than Sibree. His own letter
reconciles the two: he went up in 1828 and the Society regularised him in 1829.) Your sketch of 1834 catches him in the two
years between ordination and the trouble that ended his mission.

You may also like to know that the same picture appears to exist in original form in South
Africa: that draft entry places a **Bell watercolour of a church service at Philippolis in
1834, with Kolbe in the pulpit, in the Africana Museum in Johannesburg** — now MuseumAfrica —
reproduced in *The Journal of Andrew Smith*, edited by William F. Lye (Cape Town: Balkema,
1975). Neither catalogue seems to mention the other.

**Second, my request.** George Augustus Kolbe is my wife's family's ancestor, and we hold no
likeness of anyone in the family born before 1850. **Would it be possible to obtain a
reproduction of item 3, for private family-history use?** I would be glad to pay whatever
reproduction fee applies and to follow whatever permissions procedure you require. If a
digital copy is straightforward, that would be ideal; if not, I would be grateful simply to
know the physical dimensions and any inscription on the photograph or its mount.

If it would help, I am happy to send you the relevant page of the National Library's catalogue
entry, a transcript of the 1830 letter, and the page images of the 1833 magazine.

With thanks,
David de Franceski
