**Subject:** Enquiry — MSB751, the GA Kolbe Collection (a letter of 23 August 1830)

Dear Special Collections,

I am writing from Australia about a small manuscript collection in your care, and
I would be grateful for advice on how best to obtain a copy.

**The item.** Your catalogue entry, as it reaches me through the National
Archives' NAAIRS index, reads:

> **MSB751 — GA KOLBE COLLECTION.** Donated by Dr Jean Griffiths, Somerset West,
> 1985. Photocopy, 4 pages.
> "George Augustus Kolbe, pharmacist, 1820 settler, missionary, married Margaret
> Downing. + **Letter, 1830 Aug. 23, from Johan Kolbe, London, to his son, George
> Augustus Kolbe on his ordination.**"
> A second entry gives: "George Augustus Kolbe, 1802–44. **LMS missionary.**"

**Why I am asking.** George Augustus Kolbe is my wife's — my own — direct
ancestor. He came out with the 1820 Settlers on the *Nautilus* in Owen's party,
entered on the roll as a chemist aged twenty when he was in fact sixteen, and
died on the farm Wurtemburg in the district of Colesberg on 1 December 1844. His
father, **Johan Gottlieb Kolbe**, was a tailor who died at 34 Conduit Street,
London, on 18 November 1845 and was buried at the German Lutheran church of the
Savoy.

**I have never found a single document that shows the two men in contact.** Your
catalogue entry describes one.

**What I would like, in order.**

1. **A copy of the letter of 23 August 1830** — all four pages of the collection
   if they are one item, and the letter certainly.
2. **Whether the letter carries an address at its head.** If it does, that one
   line answers a question I have failed to answer any other way: an 1844
   obituary places the family at Brunswick-square, while the father's own will
   and death record place him in Conduit Street, off Bond Street. They are
   different parts of London and I cannot choose between them.
3. **What the rest of the four pages are** — the catalogue does not say, and
   "collection" may mean more than the letter.

**Two questions about the item itself.** The catalogue calls it a photocopy.
Would you know **where the original is**, or whether Dr Griffiths deposited
anything further? And is the description of him as an **LMS missionary** drawn
from the papers themselves or supplied by the donor? I ask because the only other
evidence I have for his mission work is a recollection written down forty-five
years after the fact, and I would like to know whether your entry is an
independent witness to it or a restatement of the same family memory.

**Practical.** I am happy to pay reproduction fees and to receive scans by email.
If an appointment and a personal visit are the only way, I would welcome a
suggestion of how an overseas enquirer should go about engaging somebody locally.
