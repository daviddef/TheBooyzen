**Subject:** Copies from CWM/LMS — four journals of George Augustus Kolbe, Philippolis, 1828–1834

Dear Special Collections,

I am researching **George Augustus Kolbe (1802–1844)**, an 1820 Settler who served the London
Missionary Society in southern Africa from 1828 to 1837, latterly at Philippolis. (He was in
the field from April 1828 by his own account, and formally accepted by the Society in February
1829 — your own Sibree register gives the second date, and his letter printed in the
*Evangelical Magazine* for 1831 gives the first.) He is my
wife's family's ancestor, and I am assembling a documented family history rather than working
towards a publication.

Your catalogue is item-level for this material, so I can give you references rather than ask
you to search. **I should be grateful for a quotation for digital copies of the following, and
for guidance on your procedure, fees and any permissions.**

**What I already hold, so that nothing is copied twice.** The *Evangelical Magazine and
Missionary Chronicle* for **1833, pp. 410–412**, prints "Extracts from the Journal of Mr. George
Augustus Kolbe, from 8th October, 1831, to 3rd September, 1832" over his signature. I have read
those three pages from the page images and transcribed them. This bears on the list above in
three distinct ways, and I would rather set them out than leave you to guess:

- **CWM/LMS/05/05/108 (6 Oct 1832 – Sep 1833) is wholly outside that window** — the printed
  extracts stop on 3 September 1832, and this journal begins a month later. **Nothing of it has
  been printed so far as I can establish, so this is now my first priority after /100.**
- **CWM/LMS/05/05/106 (1831–1834) overlaps it**, and what I want from the manuscript is
  precisely what the editor left out. The printed text is visibly cut — at one point it drops
  into square brackets to summarise him rather than quote him. **I am not asking for a copy of
  what I can already read; I am asking for the parts the Society's editor did not print.**
- **CWM/LMS/05/05/100 (the November 1828 tour) remains entirely unseen.** It was printed in
  edited form in the Society's *Transactions* for 1829, and is cited in the modern literature as
  an extract addressed to the Rev Richard Miles, dated at Philippolis 25 November 1828. **I have
  checked the Society's monthly for 1828 and 1829 page by page and he is named nowhere in
  either**, which I take to confirm that the printed version is in the *Transactions* and not in
  the *Magazine*. I have found no digitised run of the *Transactions* anywhere.

If it is simplest to begin with one item so that we both see how it goes, **please start with
CWM/LMS/05/05/100**, and if a second is possible, **/108**.

I ask for 1836 and 1837 for a particular reason, and I would rather state it plainly. A draft
Dictionary of South African Biography entry on Kolbe, by the late **Karel Schoeman**, records
that in 1836 he was **accused of adultery** with the wife of a member of his congregation; that
**H. Hendricks**, secretary of the Griqua Council, led the agitation; that **an inquiry by the
neighbouring French missionaries acquitted him**; and that he withdrew from the station in
March 1837, his connection with the Society being dissolved by the Directors on 19 June 1837 —
which last your own register confirms. **I would like to read what the Society was actually
told, rather than repeat a summary of it.** I have since consulted Schoeman's edition of the
*Griqua Records: the Philippolis Captaincy, 1825–1861* (Van Riebeeck Society, 1996), which
prints the Captaincy's own side of this, so the gap that remains is specifically **what the
Directors in London were told, and by whom**. If those two files are bulky, I would be glad
simply to know whether they contain material on the matter before committing to copying.

## One further question, which needs no searching

Sibree's *Register of Missionaries* (1923) describes **Frederick William Kolbe** (entry 522) as
"son of Rev. G. A. Kolbe (No. 278)". **I believe this to be an error** — other sources make him
a Westphalian German of the Rhenish Mission, and the two birth dates are only eight months
apart, which is impossible. **If the Society holds candidate papers for Frederick William
Kolbe on his acceptance in 1853, they would state his parentage and settle it.** I do not ask
you to look; I ask only whether such papers survive as a class, so that I know whether to
pursue it.
