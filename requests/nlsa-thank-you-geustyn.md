# Drafted — thank-you to the National Library of South Africa

**Status: DRAFTED, NOT SENT — 20 September 2026.** Sending is David's.
**To:** Melanie Geustyn, Senior Librarian, Special Collections, National Library of
South Africa — as a **reply to her email of 18 September 2026**, so that it keeps the
thread and the MSB 751 reference.

**Why this is worth sending.** The enquiry went in on 15 September and was answered on
18 September — **three days**, with the whole manuscript scanned and a second document
nobody asked for. Special Collections librarians almost never learn what a request was
*for*, or whether it came to anything. This one came to a great deal, and saying so
costs one email.

**A question this draft originally asked has been struck, because she had already
answered it.** The first version asked who compiled the unsigned DSAB typescript. Her
covering email says: *"The author, KS, would be Dr. Karel Schoeman who worked here at the
National Library."* This archive had read the two attachments and not the sentence they
were attached to. What remains is the one question she genuinely has not been asked:
whether the entry was ever published, and whether the catalogue's "LMS missionary" came
from the papers or from the donor.

**Subject:** Re: Enquiry — MSB751, the GA Kolbe Collection (a letter of 23 August 1830)

Dear Ms Geustyn,

Thank you — three days, four scanned pages and a second document I had not asked for is
more than I hoped for, and I thought you should know what it produced.

George Augustus Kolbe is my wife's family's ancestor. On the London side we had built him
a father from Westminster records — a tailor named Johan Gottlieb Kolbe, traced through
rate books, the London Gazette, the 1841 census and a burial register — but we had never
found a single document showing the two men in contact. MSB 751 is that document. It is
the first thing in our family archive that Johan Gottlieb Kolbe wrote himself; everything
else is somebody writing about him.

Some of what it settled:

- He writes of his son having been "fully accepted by the [L.M.S.] as one of their
  Missionaries", with Dr Philip carrying an earlier letter to the Cape. We had that only
  from a memoir written forty-five years afterwards.
- His son Henry is dying of dropsy and "appears at present to be resigned to the
  Catastrophe". Henry died on 13 April 1832.
- The closing sends "kind love from your Sisters & brother" — sisters plural, brother
  singular. George Augustus's 1844 obituary calls him "the last remaining of eight sons".
  That letter shows the sentence became true in April 1832, twelve years before it was
  printed.

The one thing I most hoped for, it does not give: the heading is "London" and nothing
finer, so a long-running argument in our records between Brunswick Square and Conduit
Street is still open. I have published that as plainly as the rest.

The second attachment — the draft DSAB entry — turned out to be worth as much again. It
gave us nine years of the man's life we did not have: the Graaff-Reinet society from about
1824, Boesmanskool with James Clark, the Caledon journey of November 1828, the ordination
of 25 May 1833, and the events at Philippolis in 1836 and 1837. It also corrected us —
we had repeated the catalogue's description of MSB 751 as a letter written on his
ordination, and the dates in the entry show it cannot be. And its source list has become
a work list: MOOC 6/9/35 no. 7698, the marriage certificate photocopy at the Albany
Museum, and the two portraits it names, neither of which we have seen.

I have checked it against our own records point by point, and it agrees with us on nine
things we had reached from quite different documents. **Dr Schoeman was careful** — and
thank you for telling me it was him, and for looking in the Background File to answer the
questions about the donation. A "no, there is nothing further" from someone who has
actually opened the file is worth a great deal more than silence, and I have recorded it
as an answer rather than a gap.

**Two small questions remain, and please treat both as entirely optional.**

First: **was the entry ever published in the Dictionary of South African Biography**, or is
the copy you sent me the only form it exists in? It is headed *voorlopige teks*, and the
DSAB's later volumes had a difficult history, so I would like to know whether I am reading
a published article or a draft that never ran.

Second, and this one I did ask before but I do not think the papers could answer it:
**your catalogue calls him an LMS missionary — is that drawn from the papers themselves, or
supplied by Dr Griffiths?** If it came from the donor, it is family memory and I will treat
it as such. The letter itself now settles the fact either way, so this is tidiness rather
than need.

Either way, thank you. The material is going onto a family archive that publishes what it
cannot find alongside what it can, and MSB 751 is now the oldest document on it written
by a member of the family.

With thanks,
David de Franceski
