## Why ask

**Catherine Maria Sophia Booyzen, born Barry**, died at Mayfair, Johannesburg on 5 September 1940. Her
death registration records her occupation as *Home Duties* and, on the line below,
**"Military Pensioner."**

Her husband had been dead thirty-five years and died of a stomach ulcer, so the pension is not his.
This archive's first guess was **a son killed in the First World War**. That has now been searched and
**it is wrong**:

- **No Booyzen** appears among the South African dead of 1914–18.
- Of thirteen **Booysen** deaths in those years, **none matches a son by forename**, and most are in the
  Native Labour Corps, Cape Coloured Labour Regiment and Military Labour Bureau — units not open, in the
  segregated Union Defence Force, to a family recorded as *"European"* on every certificate.
- **All her identifiable sons survived**, dying in 1932, 1952, 1956, 1966 and 1980.

⇒ **The pension must rest on a man who served and came home** — most likely a son, possibly disabled,
with his mother as a dependant. **That is a personnel and pension question, and the Documentation Centre
is the only place it can be answered.** Nothing relevant is online: FamilySearch's South African
Department of Defence collection is browse-by-image and not name-indexed.

## Practical notes for David

- **Fill in your contact details** where the draft says *[email address]* and *[postal address]* — the
  Centre will not reply into a void, and a postal address is often required for certified copies.
- **Expect months, not weeks.** The Documentation Centre is small and heavily used; genealogical
  enquiries queue behind official ones.
- **They may ask for a service number.** We have none. If they cannot search by name alone, the fallback
  is to ask them to search **the pension registers by the pensioner's name** instead — that is the
  reverse of the usual direction and is the strength of this particular request: **we know there was a
  pension, and we know her name, address and date of death exactly.**
- **Proof of descent** is sometimes required for personnel files of people who may still have living
  close relatives. The line is documented: David → Cheryl Anne Lerena → Catherine Mary Sophia Booyzen →
  George Downing Mountjoy Booyzen → **Catherine Maria Sophia Barry**. Certificates for each step exist.
- **If they hold nothing**, ask them to say so explicitly and to name the repository that would — a
  negative answer from the custodian is itself worth having, and closes the question honestly.
