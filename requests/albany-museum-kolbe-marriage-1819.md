**Why here.** The Albany Museum keeps **a genealogical archive of the 1820 British Settlers** —
which is exactly what George Augustus Kolbe was, arriving in Algoa Bay on the *Nautilus* on
14 April 1820 in Thomas Owen's party. A draft Dictionary of South African Biography entry,
compiled in February 1992, lists among its sources: **"Albany Museum, Grahamstad: fotokopie
van die huwelikssertifikaat van G.A. Kolbe (1819)."**

**What this archive has and has not.** It holds the **marriage allegation** — the sworn
statement made to obtain the licence, and the document on which the groom's age was sworn
falsely; he was sixteen. **It does not hold the marriage itself.** A certificate would give
the witnesses and the signatures, and the bride signed the allegation with a mark.

**Subject:** 1820 Settler genealogy — a photocopy of the 1819 Kolbe–Downing marriage certificate

Dear Albany Museum,

I am researching the family of **George Augustus Kolbe**, an 1820 Settler who arrived at Algoa
Bay aboard the *Nautilus* on 14 April 1820 as a member of Thomas Owen's party, described in
the ship's list as a chemist. He later served as a London Missionary Society missionary at
Boesmanskool and Philippolis, and died on the farm Wurtemberg in the district of Colesberg on
1 December 1844. He is my wife's family's ancestor.

A draft entry for the Dictionary of South African Biography, compiled in February 1992 and now
held with manuscript collection MSB 751 at the National Library of South Africa, lists among
its sources:

> Albany Museum, Grahamstad: fotokopie van die huwelikssertifikaat van G.A. Kolbe (1819)

— a photocopy of his 1819 marriage certificate.

**My question is simply whether that item is still in your collections, and if so whether I
might obtain a copy of it.** The marriage was to **Margaret Downing**, at St Leonard's,
Shoreditch, in London, on 20 January 1819. I already hold the marriage *allegation* — the
sworn statement made to obtain the licence — but not the certificate, and the two are
different documents: the certificate would give the witnesses and the signatures, which the
allegation does not.

If there is a search fee, a reproduction fee, or a form to complete, I will gladly follow
whatever procedure applies. I am writing from the United Kingdom and cannot visit in person,
so I would be grateful to know if there is a research service, or a researcher you could
recommend.

More broadly: if your 1820 Settler genealogical archive holds anything else filed under
**Kolbe**, or under **Owen's party**, I would very much like to know of it. What I have of his
first four years in the Cape is a single sentence in a secondary work.

With thanks for your time,
David de Franceski
