Two people did unpaid work for this archive on 14 September and neither has been
told what it produced. Riël Smit answered from the **1981 edition this project does
not own**; Pieter van Zyl went and looked at the notice, searched for a marriage
that does not exist, and remembered a detail that turned out to be nine lines lower
on the same sheet.

---

Riël, Pieter — thank you both. That settled it, and I want to report back rather
than just take the answer and go quiet.

**Riël, your nine words did the whole job.** My standing objection was that Pama's
revision might have moved him, in which case the 1876 death notice would stand
unopposed and I would have been arguing against a superseded reading. "de Villiers
& Pama (1981) still place Petrus Jacobus (b3c4d7) b. 21 Sept 1788 under JAN b3c4"
closes that off, and I could not have closed it myself — I have only the 1893
original.

**Pieter, you were right about the child, and I should have seen it.** You said you
thought a child of Petrus Jacobus was called Gerhardus Lodewicus. Line 12 of Notice
678 lists the eight children, all mondig, and the fifth is **JAN GERTHARDUS
LODEWIKUS BOOIJSEN**. So the name in the parents' box on line 3 is the back half of
a son's name, nine lines lower, in one clerk's hand — and the half that was dropped
is **Jan**, which is the name the book gives the father. A grandson named for his
grandfather is the ordinary thing in this family. The informant reached for the
household's own stock of names and came back carrying too much of one of them.

Your point about who does the remembering holds up here exactly. The form is
countersigned by **H. S. Booijsen** — Hendrik Stefanus, the seventh child, whose own
name is four lines above his signature. He was born about 1828. Jan married in 1770
and remarried in December 1793, and on your reckoning was dead more than forty years
before 1876, so Hendrik Stefanus was writing the name of a man he had almost
certainly never met, about a father who was eighty-eight.

And your failed search is worth as much as the finds: no marriage between a
Gerhardus Lodewicus and a Magdalena de Beer can be produced by anyone. The mother on
line 3 agrees with the book exactly. The informant got the woman right and the man
wrong, which is what someone reaching for a half-remembered pair of names would do.

**What I have not done, and why.** I have not moved Gerthardus Lodewikus off the top
of my chart. Everything against him is secondary — de Villiers 1893, and Pama
agreeing with it, is one printed genealogy agreeing with its own earlier edition,
which is not two sources agreeing — and what he has is a primary document signed by
a son. I have entered Jan Booijsen as *inferred* rather than *documented* and left
the disagreement visible on the site instead of tidying it away.

**What would end it**, and here I would welcome being told I am wrong: de Villiers
is not really a source, he is an index to one. His entry for Petrus Jacobus was
compiled from the doopregister, and that baptism entry of 21 September 1788 names
the father in a contemporary hand, written within days, by somebody with no reason
to be confused. I had assumed I could reach it through FamilySearch and I cannot —
their Dutch Reformed collection holds 54 registers for Paarl (Drakenstein), 48 for
Graaff-Reinet and 55 for Stellenbosch, and sorted earliest first **the first row in
every town is 1843**. It is the post-1842 church archive series and it does not
reach the eighteenth century at all. So it is Roeland Street, in person.

**Does either of you know whether the pre-1843 Drakenstein doopregisters have been
transcribed or filmed anywhere I have not thought of?** That is the only question I
have left on this, and if the answer is "no, go to Cape Town", that is a perfectly
good answer and I will stop looking for a shortcut.

One thing to give back, since you both work these families. Volume 3 of the 1893
edition, entry 876, gave me a woman I had carried for weeks as a birth year and five
spellings: **Willem Slier, van Hamburg, married 19 November 1780 to Anna Catharina
Fortman**, their daughter **Anna Catharina baptised 14 October 1787, married Hermanus
Pieterse**. Two pages on, **Hermanus Pietersen, parents unknown, married 5 April 1807
to Anna Catharina Slier**, with sons Willem Hermanus (1809) and Johan Christiaan
(1810). I had been running that father and son as one man, out of two documents that
spell him differently. The book separated them and handed me a half-brother I did
not know existed.
