**Searched first, so the letter does not ask for what a catalogue already answers.**
Discovery was searched on 15 September for MOUNTJOY, MONTJOY, MOUNTJOYE and
MUNTJOY across the WO series. In the name-catalogued discharge series — WO 97,
WO 119, WO 120, WO 121, WO 25 — there are exactly two, and neither is this man:

* `WO 97/1147/67` — JOHN MONTJOY, born Barnstaple, Devonshire, 56th Foot
* `WO 121/44/280` — WILLIAM MONTJOY alias WILLIAM MOUNTJOY, born Dublin, 16th and 60th Foot

**And the control says the absence is real.** The regiment is indexed under
*"21st Dragoons (Light)"* rather than *"21st Light Dragoons"* — 95 WO 97 records
in a single page of results, plus entries in WO 119, WO 121 and WO 25. So the
series is name-catalogued, the regiment is in it, and he is not.

The likeliest reason is structural: WO 97 and WO 121 are **pensioners'**
documents. A man discharged at the Cape who never claimed a Chelsea or
Kilmainham pension would leave no paper in either.

**Subject:** Tracing a soldier discharged at the Cape, 21st Dragoons (Light), 1816–1817 — is WO 12/1450 the right piece?

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am researching a family history from Australia and would be grateful for
guidance rather than a lookup. I have done the catalogue work and would like to
be told whether my reading of it is right before I pay anyone to open a box.

**The man.** JAMES MOUNTJOY, a saddler, born about 1782 somewhere in Europe. He
married at Graaff-Reinet in the Cape Colony on **9 March 1817** and died at
Cradock in **September 1827**. His Cape death notice answers the question "names
of the parents of the deceased" with **NOT KNOWN**, and his marriage register
rules a line through the column that should say where he came from and leaves it
empty — the blank was there while he stood in the room.

**The one statement of where he came from** is not a record at all. It is a
memoir printed in a Bloemfontein newspaper in November 1869 by John Montgomery,
founder of Burgersdorp, recalling his arrival at Cradock in January 1822, when
the village held about six people. He lists them, and one is:

> "James MOUNTJOY (a saddler, who was discharged from the **21st Light
> Dragoons**)" — and Montgomery goes on to describe lodging in his house and
> breakfasting with him.

There is no room in a village of six for two saddlers of one name, so I am
satisfied it is him.

**What I have searched, and what I think it means.** Discovery gives me two
Montjoys in the name-catalogued discharge series and neither is this man (the
references are above). Since the same catalogue holds a great many 21st Dragoons
(Light) men in WO 97, WO 119, WO 121 and WO 25, I take the absence to be real
rather than an indexing gap — and I assume the reason is that those are
pensioners' papers, and a man discharged at the Cape who never drew a pension
would not appear in them.

**My questions, and they are short.**

1. **Is that reading correct?** If a man was discharged locally at the Cape
   in 1816 or 1817 without a pension, is it right that WO 97 and WO 121 would
   simply not hold him?

2. **I believe the piece I want is `WO 12/1450`.** Discovery gives it as
   *21st Light Dragoons, 1816–1817*, and it sits in a continuous run for this
   regiment — WO 12/1446 (1805–06), 1447 (1807–09), 1448 (1810–12),
   1449 (1813–15), **1450 (1816–17)**, 1451 (1818), 1452 (1819–20).

   The dating I am working to: the regiment sailed from Portsmouth for the Cape
   in **May 1806**, and an extract of a letter from the Cape dated 1 and 10
   September 1817 — printed in the *Sun*, the *Star*, the *Public Ledger*, the
   *Morning Herald* and the *Hampshire Chronicle* that November — says "the 21st
   Light Dragoons have embarked for India". **He married at Graaff-Reinet on
   9 March 1817**, six months before that, so I believe he was discharged at the
   Cape and stayed behind when the regiment sailed. If that is right,
   **WO 12/1450 alone should contain the quarter he disappears from**, and his
   disappearance would date the discharge.

   *(An earlier draft of this letter named `WO 12/1430`. That is the 20th and
   21st Dragoons for 1867–1868 and I had it wrong; please disregard it.)*

3. **Do soldiers' description books survive for this regiment?** Those give age,
   height, birthplace and previous trade, and "saddler" would be a strong
   confirmation as well as a possible birthplace. Searching WO 25 in Discovery
   for this regiment returns officers' services records only, which I take to
   mean either that no description book for the 21st survives or that it is not
   catalogued by regiment — and I cannot tell which from here.

4. **How do I have it done?** I am in Australia and cannot attend. I would be
   glad of your record copying service if the item can be identified closely
   enough, or of your list of independent researchers if it cannot — and of an
   honest word if this is a bigger job than it looks.

I am conscious that overseas enquiries take time that could go to readers in
front of you, and I am grateful for whatever you can say.
