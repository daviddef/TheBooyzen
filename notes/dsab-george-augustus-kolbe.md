# The DSAB entry on George Augustus Kolbe

**What it is.** The second attachment to Melanie Geustyn's email of 18 September 2026 (the
first being MSB 751 itself). A two-page Afrikaans typescript headed:

> MSB 751 / [Konsep] vir DSAB / POSBUS 5316, 8000 KAAPSTAD / **VOORLOPIGE TEKS**

— a *preliminary text*, a draft entry for the **Suid-Afrikaanse Biografiese Woordeboek**
(Dictionary of South African Biography). Initialled **KS** and dated **18/2/92** in pen at the foot.

**The author is Karel Schoeman**, on the word of the library that holds the file. Melanie
Geustyn's covering email, written with the Background File open in front of her: *"The author,
KS, would be Dr. Karel Schoeman who worked here at the National Library."* Her *"would be"* is a
hedge and the right one — the initials are an identification, not a signature. This archive
reasoned its way to the same name from the inside and published it as an inference it would not
assert, **having read the two attachments and not the sentence they were attached to**. Logged as
a correction. The Background File holds **nothing further about the donation or about any other
deposit by Dr Griffiths**.

**What it is not.** A secondary work. This archive does not take secondary works as evidence.
Every statement below is *a claim to be tested*, not a fact adopted — with one exception noted
at the end, where the entry resolves an ambiguity rather than asserting a new fact.

## The entry, in English

**Kolbe, George Augustus** (b. London, Eng., **7.12.1802** – d. **Knapdaar**, dist. Colesberg,
**1.12.1844**), missionary of the LMS, was the son of **J.G. Kolbe, a German apothecary resident
in London**. Nothing is known of his early life, and his age is given divergently in various
documents: his death notice and his gravestone both confirm, however, that he would have been
born in **1802**.

He married at London on **20.1.1819**, at a notably youthful age, **Margaret Downing
(1803–1867)**, who by later family tradition was of noble descent. The following year they came
out to South Africa with the British Settlers as members of **Owen's party on the *Nautilus***:
Kolbe's occupation is given as **apothecary** and his age as **20**. This group of Settlers was
placed on the Kap River in the Eastern Province, but they soon dispersed, and nothing further is
known of Kolbe until his entering the service of the **Graaff-Reinetse Zendelings Genootschap**
about **1824**. He then worked for several years **among the slaves in the district of
Graaff-Reinet**, until the society could no longer afford his services and amalgamated with the
LMS.

On **8 February 1828** he was accepted by the latter body as a **mission assistant** and placed
at the newly founded station **Boesmanskool (the present Bethulie)** across the Orange, to work
with **James Clark** among the **San**. In March **1831** he was transferred to the neighbouring
Griqua station **Philippolis**, where he again worked with **J. Melvill**, and after Melvill's
departure in March 1832 alone. On **25.5.1833** he was **ordained** as a missionary by the French
missionaries **T. Arbousset** and **E. Casalis**.

At Philippolis, then still an outpost of white settlement in the interior of South Africa, Kolbe
played an important part through his practical help and moral support to other missionaries in
the region, especially the French who began work among **Moshweshwe's** Basotho in 1833, and it
was he who in 1834 encouraged the **Berlin Missionary Society** to found the station **Bethanie**
for the Korana of the Riet River. **His journey to the Caledon River valley with his colleague
Melvill in November 1828**, which apparently took them as far as the vicinity of present-day
**Ladybrand**, is the earliest journey in the present-day OFS of which a written record has
survived (*infra*).

Kolbe's work as a missionary at Philippolis was made difficult by the division between the
Griquas and the Basters on the station, of which Melvill had already been a victim, and his
position in the community was further undermined by tensions in his marriage. **In 1836 he was
accused of adultery with the wife of one of his congregation** — a charge often used as a means
of revenge against missionaries of the period. How far it rested on truth in this case can no
longer possibly be established, but other factors undoubtedly played a part in the accusation,
and **H. Hendricks**, secretary of the Griqua Council, played a leading role in the agitation
against him. **After an inquiry carried out by the neighbouring French missionaries he was
acquitted**, but he continued to meet fierce opposition from his younger colleague **T.
Atkinson**, who came to work at Philippolis in August 1836, with the result that he withdrew
from the station in March 1837, and **on 19.6.1837 he was dismissed by the LMS**.

After lingering for a time on his farm **Driefontein near Philippolis** and at **Bethulie**,
Kolbe settled on the farm **Wurthemberg (sometimes also given as Knapdaar)** in the district of
Colesberg, where he died suddenly seven years later. **He left ten children, although his death
notice records only eight, of whom two were of age, while a further three had died as infants.**
His eldest son, **J.G.E. Kolbe (1822–68)**, later lived at **Leeuberg** near Bloemfontein and
won fame as a **commandant** during the Second Sotho–Free State War. **F.W. Kolbe (1821–99) of
the Rhenish Missionary Society was not related to this family.**

**A portrait of Kolbe as a young man** is in the possession of a descendant, **C.J. du Plessis of
Ruigtevlei, district Bethulie**; it is reproduced in Olivier (*infra*). The **Africana Museum in
Johannesburg** holds a **watercolour by C. Bell of a church service at Philippolis in 1834 with
Kolbe in the pulpit**; a reproduction appears in *The journal of Andrew Smith*, ed. William F.
Lye (Cape Town: Balkema, 1975).

### Where it is wrong — and the archive can prove it

**"J.G. Kolbe, 'n Duitse apteker wat in Londen woonagtig was" — a German apothecary living in
London.** He was a **tailor**. Not by inference: by one hundred and twenty-one Westminster rate
book entries, the printed roll of 234 master tailors of 1799, the master tailors' list of April
1834, three London Gazette partnership dissolutions all reading "TAILORS", the 1841 census
occupation column, the Westminster burial register, and his own son John Henry's sworn
deposition at the Old Bailey in 1819 — **"My father is a tailor, and lives in Church-street,
Soho."**

The German is right. The London is right. The trade is the son's, moved one generation up.

**This is the whole value of the exercise.** The entry is accurate wherever its author had a
document. The single thing he had no document for is the single thing he got wrong — and this
archive can only say so because it went to Westminster and read the rate books. A secondary work
is a reading list, not a witness.

### Where it settles something

**"Wurthemberg (soms ook aangegee as Knapdaar)"** — the header of the entry gives the death place
as *Knapdaar* and the body explains why: they are **two names for one farm**. There is no conflict
between the entry and the obituary's "Wirtemberg". What this archive gains is **a second name to
search** — Knapdaar, district Colesberg, in the deeds records and NAAIRS.

### Where it corrects this archive

**MSB 751 is not a letter "on his ordination".** The NLSA catalogue described it so and this
archive repeated the catalogue. The entry dates the ordination to **25 May 1833**, nearly three
years after the letter of 23 August 1830 — and the letter itself says he had been "fully accepted
by the [L.M.S.] as one of their Missionaries". Three distinct steps now stand in order:

- **8 February 1828** — accepted as a mission **assistant**, posted to Boesmanskool
- **August 1830** — **accepted as a missionary** (the father's letter, written as fresh news)
- **25 May 1833** — **ordained**, by Arbousset and Casalis

The letter and the entry corroborate each other in a way neither could alone. Logged as a
correction.

### What is entirely new, and untested

- The **Graaff-Reinet Missionary Society from c.1824**, working **among the slaves** of the
  district. This archive had only John Montgomery's memory of a Bushman mission school at
  Koolskop about 1824. The two are not the same claim and are not yet reconciled.
- **Boesmanskool = the present Bethulie**, with **James Clark**, among the **San**, from
  8 February 1828. *Boesmanskool* is literally "Bushman school" — which is what Montgomery
  called what he saw, in English, forty-five years later. This archive has Bethulie already: it
  is where the Settler's son Frederick Fortunatus was born in 1839.
- The **November 1828 journey** to the Caledon valley with Melvill, as far as present-day
  Ladybrand — **the earliest journey in the present-day Free State with a surviving written
  record**, and it is *printed*, under Kolbe's own name (source 6).
- **Ordination 25.5.1833 by Arbousset and Casalis.**
- **The adultery accusation of 1836**, H. Hendricks's agitation, the French missionaries' inquiry,
  **the acquittal**, T. Atkinson's opposition, withdrawal March 1837 and **dismissal by the LMS on
  19 June 1837**. This archive held none of it. It is published here as *what the entry says*.
- **Driefontein, near Philippolis** — a farm this archive did not know he held.
- **Thirteen children, not eleven**: ten left, eight on the death notice, three dead as infants.
- **F.W. Kolbe (1821–99) of the Rhenish Missionary Society is not related** — a namesake
  explicitly excluded, which is worth as much as an inclusion.
- **A portrait of him as a young man exists**, with C.J. du Plessis of Ruigtevlei, dist. Bethulie,
  reproduced in Olivier.
- **A Charles Bell watercolour of him in the pulpit at Philippolis in 1834**, Africana Museum,
  Johannesburg, reproduced in Lye's *Journal of Andrew Smith*.

This archive has no likeness of anybody born before 1850. There may be two.

## What was worked from the list, 20 September 2026

- **Source 6** (Kolbe's own printed journal, 1829) — searched the Internet Archive by title and by
  full text, and Google Books. **Not found.** The Internet Archive has no run of the LMS *Quarterly
  Chronicle of Transactions*; the Books API returned HTTP 429. Left blocked, with SOAS named as the
  route to the manuscript behind it.
- **Source 5** (Sibree, 1923) — not on the Internet Archive under that title.
- **The Bethanie claim of 1834** — found repeated in a general encyclopaedia article, which footnotes
  **Schoeman, *Die Huis van die Armes* (1985)**. *Not* independent corroboration: the same author,
  seven years earlier. Recorded as such. It also all but settles who wrote the typescript.
- **New, and not in the Bronne:** **Karel Schoeman (ed.), *Griqua Records: the Philippolis Captaincy,
  1825–1861*, Van Riebeeck Society II/25** — 162 documents, including the Philippolis Law Book and
  the missionary correspondence. Published two years after this draft was typed. The place to test
  the 1836 accusation. Purchasable eBook from HIPSA; no free digitisation.
