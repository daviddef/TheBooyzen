**Not one is from Barkly East, Dordrecht, Wodehouse, Indwe, Lady Grey or Elliot.**
Kenhardt, Campbell, Koopmansfontein, Daniel's Kuil, Vaalgras, Blauwputs are **Northern Cape and
Griqualand West** names; Aberdeen is the Karoo. This is a **different Booysen cluster**, two to five

⇒ <span>Negative result</span> **No Booysen from this family's district appears on the Cape Rebel roll.**

One tease: **DAVID SCHALK BOOYSEN of Kapteinskop.** *David Schalk* is a name that runs in this family —
Willem Hermanus's brother (1848–1942) and his son (1907–1966) both bore it. **But the district is wrong**,
and the Booysen surname is common enough that a shared forename pair proves nothing on its own.

## 2. BOOYZEN — zero records, in all fifteen registers
Cape Rebels 0 · Joiners 0 · Farm List 0 · Burger Deaths 0 · POWs 0 · everything 0.

**Read this cautiously.** *Booyzen* is **83 times rarer** than *Booysen*; its absence from a
12,792-name index is exactly what base rates predict, and proves nothing by itself. It is *consistent*
with a spelling adopted during or after the war — wartime clerks would have written the old form — but
it is not evidence for it.

## 3. The Joiners register — one Booysen, and he is not ours
**BOOYSEN, J.J., HEILBRON.** Heilbron is in the **Orange Free State**, not the Cape.

The testimony says *"there were some Booysens who were joiners, fighting on the side of the British."*
The national register of **952 joiners contains exactly one Booysen** — a real man, but a Free State
burgher, not a Cape colonist and not from this district. **The claim is neither confirmed nor refuted.**

# 4. THE RESULT THAT MATTERS: D. W. SCHOEMAN OF CAERLAVEROCK

Searching the register on the **farm name** rather than the surname returned **two Cape Rebels at
Caerlaverock**:

| | |
|---|---|
| **SCHOEMAN, DANIEL WILLEM** — ID **8434** | Occupation **PLAASBOER** · **Trial date: JUNIE 1901** · Address **CAERLAVEROCK** · District **WODEHOUSE – BARKLEY-OOS** |
| **KLOPPER, CHRISTIAAN F.** — ID **4721** | Occupation **PLAASBOER** · **Trial date: JUNIE 1901** · Address **CAERLAVEROCK** · District **WODEHOUSE – BARKLEY-OOS** |

Now set that beside the marriage register already transcribed by this archive:

> **8 July 1901** — Petrus Jacobus Pretorius married Magrita Wilhelmina Elizabeth Barry
> *"in **woonhuis D. Schoeman** te **Caerlaverock**"*, in the district of **Barkly Oost**,
> witnessed and signed by **D. W. SCHOEMAN**.

### The finding
**Daniel Willem Schoeman, farmer of Caerlaverock, was tried as a Cape Rebel in June 1901. The family
married in his house the following month, and he signed the register as a witness.**

<span>Documented</span> This is the strongest corroboration the family's rebel testimony has ever had.
Until now the archive could say only that the family *lived in a rebel district*. It can now name
**the man, the farm, the district and the month of his treason trial** — and show the family in his
house, at his table, weeks later.

A **second** rebel, **Christiaan F. Klopper**, was a farmer at the same place and tried in the same month.
Caerlaverock was not incidentally connected to the rebellion; **it was a rebel farm.**

### What it does NOT establish
1. **It still does not put a Booysen or a Barry on a rebel roll.** The rebel here is the host, not the
   family. The testimony's claim that *"they were rebels"* remains uncorroborated **for the family
   itself**.
2. **It does not prove he was "Commandant D. Schoeman"** of Labuschagne's Nek, March 1900. The record's
   *Commando* field reads **N/A**, and there is a **cluster** of D. W. Schoemans tried in the same
   division:
   - **Daniel Wilhelmus Schoeman**, plaasboer, **Wolwekloof**, Dordrecht–Wodehouse, tried **1 Mei 1901**
     *(entered twice — IDs 8429 and 8430)*
   - **Daniel W. Schoeman**, plaasboer, **Waschbank**, Dordrecht–Wodehouse, tried **1 Mei 1901**
   - **Danie J. W. Schoeman**, Dordrecht, tried **21 Julie 1902**
   - **D. J. Schoeman**, no address
   These may be relatives, or the same man double-entered under different farms. **Unresolved.**
3. A trial in June 1901 with **no sentence recorded** is consistent with **Class 2** treatment —
   rank-and-file rebels, disenfranchised for five years rather than imprisoned — which would explain how
   he was at home hosting a wedding in July. **Inferred, not documented.**
