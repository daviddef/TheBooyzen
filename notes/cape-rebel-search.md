Kenhardt, Campbell, Koopmansfontein, Daniel's Kuil, Vaalgras, Blauwputs are **Northern Cape and

⇒ <span>Negative result</span> **No Booysen from this family's district appears on the Cape Rebel roll.**

One tease: **DAVID SCHALK BOOYSEN of Kapteinskop.** *David Schalk* is a name that runs in this family —
Willem Hermanus's brother (1848–1942) and his son (1907–1966) both bore it. **But the district is wrong**,
and the Booysen surname is common enough that a shared forename pair proves nothing on its own.

## 2. BOOYZEN — zero records, in all fifteen registers
Cape Rebels 0 · Joiners 0 · Farm List 0 · Burger Deaths 0 · POWs 0 · everything 0.

**Read this cautiously.** *Booyzen* is **83 times rarer** than *Booysen*; its absence from a
12,792-name index is exactly what base rates predict, and proves nothing by itself. It is *consistent*
with a spelling adopted during or after the war — wartime clerks would have written the old form — but
it is not evidence for it.

## 3. The Joiners register — one Booysen, and he is not ours
**BOOYSEN, J.J., HEILBRON.** Heilbron is in the **Orange Free State**, not the Cape.

The testimony says *"there were some Booysens who were joiners, fighting on the side of the British."*
The national register of **952 joiners contains exactly one Booysen** — a real man, but a Free State
burgher, not a Cape colonist and not from this district. **The claim is neither confirmed nor refuted.**

# 4. THE RESULT THAT MATTERS: D. W. SCHOEMAN OF CAERLAVEROCK

Searching the register on the **farm name** rather than the surname returned **two Cape Rebels at
Caerlaverock**:

| | |
|---|---|
| **SCHOEMAN, DANIEL WILLEM** — ID **8434** | Occupation **PLAASBOER** · **Trial date: JUNIE 1901** · Address **CAERLAVEROCK** · District **WODEHOUSE – BARKLEY-OOS** |
| **KLOPPER, CHRISTIAAN F.** — ID **4721** | Occupation **PLAASBOER** · **Trial date: JUNIE 1901** · Address **CAERLAVEROCK** · District **WODEHOUSE – BARKLEY-OOS** |

Now set that beside the marriage register already transcribed by this archive:

> **8 July 1901** — Petrus Jacobus Pretorius married Magrita Wilhelmina Elizabeth Barry
> *"in **woonhuis D. Schoeman** te **Caerlaverock**"*, in the district of **Barkly Oost**,
> witnessed and signed by **D. W. SCHOEMAN**.

### The finding
**Daniel Willem Schoeman, farmer of Caerlaverock, was tried as a Cape Rebel in June 1901. The family
married in his house the following month, and he signed the register as a witness.**

<span>Documented</span> This is the strongest corroboration the family's rebel testimony has ever had.
Until now the archive could say only that the family *lived in a rebel district*. It can now name
**the man, the farm, the district and the month of his treason trial** — and show the family in his
house, at his table, weeks later.

A **second** rebel, **Christiaan F. Klopper**, was a farmer at the same place and tried in the same month.
Caerlaverock was not incidentally connected to the rebellion; **it was a rebel farm.**

### What it does NOT establish
1. **It still does not put a Booysen or a Barry on a rebel roll.** The rebel here is the host, not the
   family. The testimony's claim that *"they were rebels"* remains uncorroborated **for the family
   itself**.
2. **It does not prove he was "Commandant D. Schoeman"** of Labuschagne's Nek, March 1900. The record's
   *Commando* field reads **N/A**, and there is a **cluster** of D. W. Schoemans tried in the same
   division:
   - **Daniel Wilhelmus Schoeman**, plaasboer, **Wolwekloof**, Dordrecht–Wodehouse, tried **1 Mei 1901**
     *(entered twice — IDs 8429 and 8430)*
   - **Daniel W. Schoeman**, plaasboer, **Waschbank**, Dordrecht–Wodehouse, tried **1 Mei 1901**
   - **Danie J. W. Schoeman**, Dordrecht, tried **21 Julie 1902**
   - **D. J. Schoeman**, no address
   These may be relatives, or the same man double-entered under different farms. **Unresolved.**
3. A trial in June 1901 with **no sentence recorded** is consistent with **Class 2** treatment —
   rank-and-file rebels, disenfranchised for five years rather than imprisoned — which would explain how
   he was at home hosting a wedding in July. **Inferred, not documented.**

### Still to do
- The **Boer Officers register (514 records)** reports **2 Schoemans** on a surname search, but the
  filtered list could not be displayed. **Whether either is the commandant is unresolved.**
- The museum's **Farm List (10,282 records)** — the burned-farm returns — has not been searched for
  Caerlaverock, Manorowen, Wolwekloof or the Booysens.
- **Barry** was seen in the database's own front-page sample of head families. Not yet searched.

# 5. The Farm List, searched — and why the result is VOID

**Searched:** `Caerlaverock`, `Manorowen`, `Wolwekloof`, `Booysen`.

| Term | Farm List | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| **Caerlaverock** | **0** | Cape Rebels **2** (Schoeman, Klopper) |
| **Manorowen** | **0** | **0 in all fifteen registers** |
| Wolwekloof | 0 | Cape Rebels (Schoeman ×2) |
| Booysen | 1 | — |

At first sight that reads as a negative result for the burned-farm testimony. **It is not a result at all.**

## What the Farm List actually is

The single Booysen record resolves to:

> **Booysen, Petrus Gerhardus · Farm Name: Blydschap · Number: 316 · District: KROONSTAD ·
> Morgen: 2002 · SRds: 56 · Number of Owners: N/A**

Those fields — *farm number, morgen, square roods, number of owners* — are a **land and property roll**,
not a record of destruction. **It is not the farm-burning return this archive assumed it might be.**

## And it does not cover the Cape at all

Sampled **≈1,280 records** drawn from across the full 10,282 via the site's own REST endpoint, taking the
district from each record's slug. **Every district returned is in the Orange Free State:**

**Winburg · Fauresmith · Vrede · Rouxville · Bethlehem · Bloemfontein · Hoopstad · Kroonstad ·
Philippolis** — and nothing else. Nine districts, no Cape entries.

A control confirms it: **"Dordrecht" returns 670 Cape Rebels and 0 Farm List records.** A major Cape
district is wholly absent from the register.

> ⇒ **The Farm List is an Orange Free State land register.** Caerlaverock and Manorowen are **Cape**
> farms. Their absence from it carries **no information whatever** — it is a category error, not a
> finding, and this archive should not present it as one.

## What follows
1. **The burned-farm claim remains completely untested.** This database was never capable of testing it.
2. The right sources are elsewhere: **Cape Colony compensation claims**, the post-war **farm-burning
   returns**, and the Cape's own **refugee-camp and district records**. None has been touched.
3. **Manorowen returns nothing in any of the fifteen registers** — so John Barry's farm, unlike
   Caerlaverock, has no wartime record here at all.

*(An incidental note: **Philippolis** appears among the Free State districts — the LMS mission station
where George Augustus Kolbe's daughter Harriet Hachilah was born in 1831.)*

- **Fred J. Barry** farmed **Ivanhoe**, Barkly East/Wodehouse, **tried June 1901** — the same district

**Identity not proven** (no birth dates or parents in the register). Fred J. does not match this family's
*Frederick Fortunatus* on the middle initial.

**Jerpoint** — an abbey in County Kilkenny. Joins **Ivanhoe**, **Manorowen** (Pembrokeshire) and
**Caerlaverock** (Dumfriesshire). It is the **first Irish place name found near the Barrys**, whose
Irish origin the archive has never been able to place.

## 2. The joiners register: ONE Booysen, in the Free State
ABW Museum **Joiners** register, 952 names. `Booysen` → **1**: **J.J. BOOYSEN, Heilbron**, from the
*Vrystaatse Argieflys*. Heilbron is ~400 miles from Barkly East and was a different country.
`Booyzen` → **0**.

Does not kill the motive — joiner lists are incomplete, and a family need only have *believed* the
association existed. But **"some Booysens" is not what the record shows.**

## 3. The camps: searched, not found
The ABW Museum has **no camp register** — this archive's queue named the wrong source. The right one is
**UCT's *British Concentration Camps of the South African War 1900–1902*.** Searched:

| Query | Result |
|---|---|
| **Booyzen** | **5** — all **Transvaal**: Gert Jacobus (2, d. Johannesburg camp, farm Rietvallei, Marico); Gertruida Magdalena (19, d. Vereeniging camp, farm Rietvlei, Heidelberg); Jan Gerhardus Lodewicus (2) |
| **Barry** | **0** in the entire database |
| Booysen + Catherine / Kathleen / John Barry / Willem Hermanus | **0 each** |
| Booyzen + Catharina | **0** |
| Booysen (all) | 334 — none matching this family's given names |

**Caveat that matters:** the UCT database is built largely from camp **death lists** and surviving
registers. It records who *died* far better than who was interned and walked out. **Absence is weak
evidence here.** The claim is *unsupported*, not disproved.

Note the coincidence, and do not build on it: a **Jan Gerhardus Lodewicus Booyzen** died aged 2 — the
same distinctive three-part name as the seventh child on the 1863 death notice, and carrying
*Gerthardus Lodewikus* from the top of the male line. He is a Transvaal child, not ours.
