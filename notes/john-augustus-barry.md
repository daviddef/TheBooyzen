# John Augustus Barry I (1819–1903) — the Irishman born at sea

The single most documented, and most contradictory, person in the Booyzen ancestry. He is the reason
the family carries English forenames, and — if the family testimony holds — a plausible root of its
anti-British politics.

## Birth: three answers, none settled
The tree records his birthplace literally as **"?Sunderland, Durham, England? Ireland? High Seas?"**
An extended research note (author unattributed, carried in the tree) sets out why:

- **Death certificate 1** — made out by his youngest son **Pieter Richard Barry**.
- **Death certificate 2** — recorded by the **Cape Mounted Rifles**, then the authority in that district,
  stationed at **Johnson's Leap**. It states John Barry was **"an Irishman born in Ireland."**
  Dated 16/10/1903 in one telling, 26/10/1903 in another — *two months after his death.*
- **A third document, dated 29/03/1915**, gives birthplace and nationality as
  **"at sea (exact whereabouts unknown), British."** Signed by the second son **James Henry Barry**,
  who died at Manor Owen on 23/06/1939 aged 92.
- That 1915 document also names **his father as John Barry** and **his mother's maiden name as "Neagle"**
  — i.e. almost certainly **Nagle**, an Irish surname of Co. Cork.

The note's own conclusion is a question, and the archive should keep it as one:

> "If then John Barry was born on the High Seas with the **1820 Settlers** en route to South Africa,
> who were his parents?"

**A birth on 8 June 1819 does not fit the 1820 Settler voyages**, which sailed late 1819–early 1820 and
arrived at Algoa Bay from April 1820. Either the birth date or the 1820 Settler hypothesis is wrong.
This is a tractable, checkable problem — see `notes/QUEUE.md`.

## The line that changes everything

> "As an Irishman, John Barry was reputed to be **anti English**, and as his son and grandson were
> married to Dutch girls **their activity during the Boer War would be interesting**."

That is written by an earlier researcher, not by us — but it lands exactly on the Booyzen name-change
testimony. His daughter **Kathleen Barry married Willem Hermanus Booyzen**, the man recorded as changing
*Booysen* to *Booyzen* to avoid being taken for a British-siding "joiner." An anti-English Irish
grandfather at Barkly East is precisely the household in which Cape Rebel sympathies would be ordinary.

**This is inference, not proof.** It is, however, the most testable inference in the archive.

## Life
- **Occupations recorded, five ways:** Carpenter and Builder · Carpenter/Builder ·
  *"timmerman en bouwvakker"* (Dutch: carpenter and building worker) · Grocer · Labourer.
  A working tradesman, not a merchant prince.
- m. **circa 23 Feb 1846, Dutch Reformed Church, Colesberg** — Margaretha Wilhelmina Elizabeth Mary
  **Kolbe**. The record carries the notation **"b3"** — a *Suid-Afrikaanse Geslagsregisters* (SAG)
  generation code, meaning she is the third child of the second generation of her Kolbe line. That code
  is a direct pointer into the published Cape genealogies.
  - An Irish/English Anglican-descended carpenter marrying into a Cape Dutch family **in the DRC** —
    he married into Afrikanerdom, not the other way round.
- **d. 25 Aug 1903 at "Manor Owen", Barkly East** — a named farm, and the family seat: his son
  James Henry died there too, in 1939.

## The children — and the geography of a district
Burgersdorp (1847, 1848, 1850, 1851, 1855, 1863) · Dordrecht (1857) · **Barkly East** (1859, 1861) ·
Lady Grey (1866). A family circulating inside one corner of the north-eastern Cape.

- John Augustus II (1847–1935) — m. Alida Jacoba van Ellinckhuyzen, 1870, DRC Colesberg
- James Henry (1848–1939) — m. Maria Margaretha Hester Catherina **Louw**, 1867, Dordrecht
- George Augustus (1850–1933)
- Margaretha Louisa Maria (1851–1915) — m. Thomas **Bedford**, 1875, Colesberg
- **Catherina Maria Sophia (29 Jul 1855 – 30 Jul 1859, Burgersdorp)** — died aged 4
- Hendrik Nicholaas (1857–1931) — m. Anna Johanna Maria **Schoemann**, 1877, Barkly East
- **Catherine Maria Sophia "Kathleen" (4 Aug 1859 – 1940)** — born **five days after her sister died**,
  and given her name. → m. Willem Hermanus Booyzen, 1879
- Willem Frederick Thomas "Tom" (1861–1890) — m. Hendrina Petronella Jacomina **Dry**, 1886; d. aged 29
- Frederick Fortunatus (1863–1952) — the forename later given to **Frederik Fortsenatus Booÿsen** (1899)
- Richard Peter (1866–1946)

Note the marriages: **Louw, van Ellinckhuyzen, Schoemann, Dry, Bedford.** Within one generation the
Irish carpenter's children had married almost entirely into Cape Dutch families.

## One record that is certainly wrong
**"Census, 12 Jan 1852, Biddulph, Huron, Canada West, Canada."** John Augustus Barry was in Burgersdorp
in 1851 and 1855, with children born there. This is a different John Barry, wrongly attached by an
automated match. **Reject.**
