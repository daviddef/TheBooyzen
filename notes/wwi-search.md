# Searching the First World War records for Kathleen Barry's sons
Searched 9 September 2026. **Result: a clean negative, and it usefully narrows the question.**

## Why the question exists
**Kathleen Barry's 1940 death certificate records her as a "MILITARY PENSIONER."** Her husband had been
dead thirty-five years and died of a stomach ulcer, so it is not his service. The archive's working guess
was **a son killed in the First World War**, with a dependant's pension attaching to the mother.

### The reading
1. **Not one matches a son by forename.** There is no John Barry, Willem Hermanus, Henry James, David
   Kolbe, Frederik Fortsenatus, Petrus, Jacobus, Forde or Alfred among them.
2. **Most are in units that were not open to this family.** In the segregated Union Defence Force the
   **South African Native Labour Corps**, the **Cape Coloured Labour Regiment**, the **Military Labour
   Bureau/Corps** and the **Indian and Malay Corps** were for black and coloured South Africans. Every
   civil document this archive has read records this family as **"European"** — Willem Hermanus's 1905
   death, Kathleen's 1940 death, Anna's 1944 death, George's 1947 death. Those men are not her sons.
3. The only forename echo is **"Fred BOOYSENS", Military Labour Bureau, died Tanzania 1916.** The unit is
   wrong for this family and Frederik Fortsenatus would have been **seventeen**. **Not a match.**

### And the decisive point
**All of Kathleen's identifiable sons survived the war** — d. 1932, 1952, 1956, 1966, 1980. A dependant's
pension for a *killed* son does not fit this family at all.

⇒ <span>Negative result</span> **No son of Kathleen Barry is among the South African war dead of the
First World War.** The archive's working hypothesis is **wrong**, or at least wrongly framed.

## What that leaves
The pension must come from a man who **served and survived**, or from another entitlement entirely:
- a **son who served and was disabled**, with his mother as a dependant;
- a **service pension** to a living son on whom she was dependent;
- or a route the archive has not thought of.

## Sources tried and their limits
- **The South Africa War Graves Project** — searched in full for both spellings. Covers the **war dead
  only**; it cannot show a man who served and came home, which is now the likelier case.
- **The Commonwealth War Graves Commission** — **not searched**: the site is behind an automated bot
  check, which this archive did not attempt to circumvent. It draws on substantially the same casualty
  data as the above.
- **FamilySearch** — its *South Africa, Department of Defence* military collection is **not name-indexed**;
  surname searches filtered to South African military records return nothing relevant. It is
  browse-by-image, so it cannot be searched this way.

## Where the answer actually is
**The SANDF Documentation Centre, Pretoria** (Personnel Archives and Reserves, 20 Visagie Street) holds
the Union Defence Force **attestation papers, personnel files and pension files** from 1912 onward.
**None of it is online.** That is where a surviving son's service — and his mother's pension — would be
recorded, and it needs a written request rather than a search.
