# The Barry line above 1819 is not sound — and the archive must say so

The MyHeritage record for **John Barry (1796–1853)**, father of John Augustus Barry I, is internally
impossible. It appears to be **two different men merged into one profile**.

## The contradictions, as recorded

| Fact in the tree | Points to |
|---|---|
| Born **1796, "Cork, Ireland (we believe this to be)"** | Ireland |
| Christened **26 Jan 1796, Bilsdale Midcable, North Riding, Yorkshire** | England |
| Residence **6 Jun 1841, Holly Bower, Bilsdale, Yorkshire** | England |
| Residence + Occupation **30 Mar 1851, Holly Bower, Bilsdale — "Farmer"** | England (1851 census) |
| **Burial 3 Jan 1853, St Hilda's, Bilsdale, Yorkshire** | England |
| **Death 8 Jan 1853, de Bruins Poort, Eastern Cape, South Africa** | South Africa |

**He is buried in Yorkshire five days before he dies in the Eastern Cape.** That is not a transcription
slip; it is two lives stitched together.

The death fact is additionally annotated **"Age About 19–20"** — for a man born in 1796 dying in 1853,
who would be 57.

## What is probably going on

A **Yorkshire farmer of Bilsdale** — christened at St Hilda's 1796, at Holly Bower in the 1841 and 1851
censuses, buried at St Hilda's in January 1853 — has been merged with a **John Barry who died at de
Bruins Poort in the Eastern Cape**. The English census-and-parish trail is coherent on its own. The
Cape death is coherent on its own. They are not the same person.

## Why it matters

Everything the family believes about being **Irish** rests above this join:
- John Augustus Barry I's death certificate calling him **"an Irishman born in Ireland"**
- his mother's maiden name **Neagle** (Nagle — Co. Cork)
- his second son **James Barry, b. 8 June 1819, County Cork**
- and, downstream, the **anti-English** reputation that makes the Cape Rebel story coherent

If the Yorkshire man is the wrong father, the Irish origin is *not thereby disproved* — the Cork
evidence comes from a different direction (the 1915 death-notice testimony and James's birthplace).
But the **pedigree link is unproven**, and the archive must show it as a break, not a chain.

## Also unsound on this profile
- Parents recorded as **"Unknown Barry"** and **"Unknown Barry"**; a brother as
  **"Other Barry's - South Africa Barry"**. These are placeholders, not people. The line **ends** here.
- **Two sons both born 8 June 1819** — John Augustus (?Sunderland/Ireland/High Seas) and
  James (County Cork). Either twins born in different countries, or one man duplicated.
  Given John Augustus's own birthplace is unresolved, **duplication is the likelier reading**, and would
  mean the "County Cork" birthplace belongs to *him*.

## Status assigned
**Disputed.** The Barry line is documented and strong **from John Augustus Barry I (1819–1903) forward**.
Above him it is a knot, and it is drawn as one.
