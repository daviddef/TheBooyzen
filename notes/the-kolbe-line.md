# Kolbe — a German tailor in Soho, and a farm called Wurtemburg

## Johan Gottlieb "John Paul" Kolbe (1765–1845)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | 1765, **Württemberg, Germany** |
| **Trade** | **Tailor**, of **34 Conduit Street, Bond Street, Middlesex** (London) |
| **Will** | written of *"JOHN GOTTLOB KOLBE of Conduit Street … in the county of Middlesex Tailor"* |
| **Married** | 1793, London — **Mary Larman** (1768–1814) |
| **Died** | **18 Nov 1845, 34 Lamb's Conduit Street, London** |
| **Buried** | **St John the Baptist, Leamington, Warwickshire** |

He appears in the record under **three versions of his own name** — *Johan Gottlieb*, *John Gottlob*,
*John Paul* — which is what assimilation looks like in a parish register. His eldest daughter's married
surname is recorded as **Kohlbeck**: she married **George Kottlich Kohlbeck** at St Anne's Soho in 1811,
which suggests the family's German original was closer to *Kohlbeck* than *Kolbe*.

## A German Lutheran London
At least **five of his children died as infants or young** and were buried at
**"St Marien's Children's Churchyard, The Strand"** — the burial ground of the **German Lutheran church
in the Strand**. Charles Samuel (b. 1806, d. 1807), Edward (b. and d. 15 Nov 1808), Frederick Charles
(d. 1809), Frederica Carolina (b. 1813, d. 1814), and John Henry (d. 1832) are all recorded there, as is
**Mary Larman herself, d. 2 Aug 1814 at St Marien's, The Strand**.

The living children were christened at **St Anne's, Soho** — the Anglican parish. German by burial,
English by baptism. A family standing in both.

## The detail that ties two continents together
George Augustus Kolbe — the son who sailed on the *Nautilus* in 1819 — **died in 1844 on a Karoo farm
named "Wurtemburg"**, in the field-cornetcy of Rhenosterberg, district Colesberg.

**He named his farm after his father's homeland.** A tailor's son from Bond Street, three years dead
before his own father, buried at Burgersdorp under a Württemberg name in the Cape Colony.

## The line, end to end
**Württemberg (1765)** → **Soho and Bond Street, London (1793–1819)** → ***Nautilus*, Gravesend,
3 Dec 1819** → **Algoa Bay, 14 Apr 1820** → **Uitenhage, Graaff-Reinet, Philippolis, Bethulie,
Burgersdorp, Colesberg** → **Barkly East** → **Johannesburg (1900s)** → **Brisbane**.

Seven moves in seven generations.
