# The Name Change — why *Booyzen* and not *Booysen*

**Source:** family testimony recorded on the MyHeritage profile of Willem Hermanus "William Henry"
Booyzen (1851–1905), attributed to **Tersia Booyzen**. MyHeritage records his **"Former name: Booysen"**
as a formal Name Change fact.

## The account, verbatim

> "According to my dad the story goes that they were living in the Cape Colony and were rebels fighting
> on the side of the Boer republics at the turn of the century but there were some Booysens who were
> joiners, fighting on the side of the British, so our great grandfather changed the spelling to Booyzen
> so as not to be associated with them. Our great great grandfather was shot and killed by the British
> and all the women and children were put in a concentration camp and the family farm in the Eastern Cape
> was burned to the ground with Kitchener's scorched earth policy. After the war our great great
> grandmother (with nothing but a pumpkin to eat) put herself and her kids on a train to Johannesburg
> where the boys initially all got jobs on the mines to make ends meet."

## Why this matters

This single paragraph explains the surname, the geography, and the family's arrival in Johannesburg.
It is the spine of the archive. It is also **testimony, not document** — and it carries at least four
claims that are individually checkable against the record:

| Claim | Status | How it can be tested |
|---|---|---|
| The family were **Cape Rebels** | untested | Cape Colony treason/rebel registers; Cape Rebel lists |
| Some Booysens were **joiners** (fought for the British) | untested | National Scouts / Orange River Colony Volunteers rolls |
| The spelling was changed **to dissociate** from them | testimony; the *fact* of the change is recorded | DRC baptism registers show which spelling, when |
| A forebear was **shot and killed by the British** | contradicted-in-part (see below) | death notices (NAAIRS/MOOC); casualty rolls |
| Women and children in a **concentration camp** | untested | Anglo-Boer War camp registers |
| The **farm burned** under Kitchener's scorched earth | untested | Compensation claims; farm-burning returns |
| **Train to Johannesburg; boys onto the mines** | untested but consistent with the 1901–07 birthplaces | — |

## The chronological problem — flag it, don't hide it

Willem Hermanus is recorded as dying **13 September 1905 at Indwe** — *after* the war (1899–1902), and
the tree itself annotates that date **"To be confirmed, not sure."** So he is not obviously the man who
was "shot and killed by the British."

Compounding it, MyHeritage flags a **consistency error**: his son **David Schalk Booyzen was born 1907**,
two years *after* the recorded death. Either the 1905 death date is wrong, or that child's parentage is.

The narrator, Tersia Booyzen, speaks of "our great grandfather" (who changed the spelling) and "our great
great grandfather" (who was shot) as **two different men in two different generations**. Resolving which
generation each refers to — and from whose vantage point Tersia was counting — is an open question.

**Working hypothesis (inferred, not documented):** the spelling change belongs to Willem Hermanus
(1851–1905), who is the man the tree actually records changing it. If so, the man "shot and killed"
would be his father **Petrus Jacobus Booysen** — but the tree gives Petrus Jacobus's death as **1884**,
fifteen years before the war. Both cannot stand. See `notes/QUEUE.md`.

## The orthography is genuinely unstable in this family

Within a single sibling group the tree records **Booysen**, **Booyzen**, and **Booÿsen** (with the
diaeresis — older Cape Dutch). That is not carelessness in the tree; it reflects registers that
themselves varied. It also means the "change" may have been a *hardening* of one variant already in
use rather than an invention.
