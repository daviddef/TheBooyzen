# The 1820 Settler is Kolbe, not Barry

The family's research notes wonder whether **John Augustus Barry** was "born on the High Seas with the
1820 Settlers." He was not — or at least, nothing shows it. **But the family does have a documented
1820 Settler, and the record for him is unusually complete.** He is on the other side of the marriage:
**George Augustus Kolbe.**

## George Augustus Kolbe (1802–1844)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | 7 Dec 1802, **Westminster St Anne, London** |
| **Christened** | 2 Feb 1803, **St Anne's, Soho, Westminster** |
| **Married** | **20 Jan 1819, St Leonard's, Shoreditch, London** — Margaret Georgina Elizabeth **Downing** |
| **Emigrated** | departed **Gravesend, 3 December 1819**, on the ship ***Nautilus*** |
| **Party** | **Thomas Owen's party** ("Owen's Party") |
| **Arrived** | **Algoa Bay, 14 April 1820** |
| **Occupations** | **Reverend Missionary, London Missionary Society** · **Chemist** · **Tailor** |
| **Died** | 1 Dec 1844, the farm **"Wurtemburg"**, field-cornetcy of **Rhenosterberg, Knapdaar, District Colesberg** |
| **Buried** | Dec 1844, **Burgersdorp Old Cemetery** |
| **Estate** | **KAB: MOOC 6/9/35: 7698 (1845)** — Cape Archives, Master's Office |

He was **sixteen years old** when he married at Shoreditch, and **seventeen** when he landed at
Algoa Bay. His wife Margaret Downing was about fifteen at the marriage.

The burial record carries a Dutch note: *"Zijn vrouw is later bijgezet in het graf"* — **"his wife was
later laid in the same grave."** She outlived him by twenty-three years.

## The children map a missionary's itinerary
Not a settler farm but a circuit — and two of the stops are **London Missionary Society stations**:

| Child | Born | Place |
|---|---|---|

| John | 1834–1835 | died an infant |
| Charlotte Felicia (twin) (Snook) | 1836 | Burgersdorp |

| Frederik Fortunatus | 1839 | **Bethulie, Orange Free State** — mission station |

The forenames are extraordinary — *Hachilah Machilah*, *Gerson Eliezar*, *Casimir*, *Fortunatus* — and
they read exactly as a missionary's children's names would: scriptural, Latinate, deliberate.
**Frederik Fortunatus Kolbe (1839)** is the origin of **Frederick Fortunatus Barry (1863)** and in turn
of **Frederik Fortsenatus Booÿsen (1899)** — one name carried across three surnames and sixty years.

## What this settles, and what it doesn't
- **Settled:** the family has a documented 1820 Settler line, by ship, party and date.
- **Settled:** it arrives through **Kolbe → Barry → Booyzen**, i.e. through two successive maternal lines.
- **Not settled:** whether the "born on the High Seas with the 1820 Settlers" story attached to
  John Augustus Barry is a garbled memory of *this* voyage, transferred down the wrong branch.
  It is a plausible reading and nothing more. **John Augustus Barry was born 8 June 1819 — six months
  before the *Nautilus* sailed** — so he cannot have been born on that ship.
