# The Mountjoys of Barkly Oost — identified
Researched 9 September 2026. **The lead is resolved, and it adds a whole branch.**

## The lead
Reading the 1889 Doop Register of **Barkly Oost** for a Booyzen baptism, the archive noticed — on the
**facing page**, three weeks later — an entry it had no reason to expect:

> **6 October 1889 · George**, geb. 21 Julie 1889.
> *Ouders:* **Hercules Johannes MOUNTJOY** and **Maria Sophia van Niekerk**.
> *Getuigen:* Jan Dirk Abraham Francois van Niekerk · Isabella Margaretha van Niekerk ·
> **Jakobus Jonathan MOUNTJOY** · Maria Kuny · **Maria Sophia MOUNTJOY**.

This line descends from **Johanna Catharina Mountjoy (1819–1863)**, who married Petrus Jacobus Booysen at
Cradock in 1838 — and of whose family the archive had little more than a surname and a father who died in
1827. Three named Mountjoys, in the right district, twenty-six years after her death.

## The chain, established
Traced through the **1820 Settlers database** (1820settlers.com) and the **FamilySearch Family Tree**,
against **South Africa, Civil Marriage Records** and **DRC registers**.

### It fits to the month
Hercules Johannes married **8 October 1888 at Dordrecht**; his first child **George was born 21 July
1889** and baptised at Barkly Oost on **6 October 1889**. FamilySearch independently records that child
as **George Mountjoy, 1889–1963**.

## The finding
> **The Mountjoys of Barkly Oost are the descendants of Johanna Catharina Mountjoy's brother.**

**George Mountjoy, saddle maker of Cradock**, was her younger brother. His sons **James Jonathan** and
**Hercules Johannes** are the two Mountjoy men on that register page — witness and father.

And the closeness is startling once you set the two baptisms side by side:

| | Booyzen | Mountjoy |
|---|---|---|
| Child | **Johanna Catharina Booyzen**, b. 20 June 1889 | **George Mountjoy**, b. 21 July 1889 |
| Baptised | **15 September 1889**, Barkly Oost | **6 October 1889**, Barkly Oost |
| Father | Willem Hermanus Booysen | Hercules Johannes Mountjoy |
| Godparents | **John Barry and Margaret W. E. Kolby** (her grandparents) | van Niekerk and Mountjoy kin |

**The two children were second cousins, born a month apart and baptised three weeks apart in the same
church.** Their fathers were first cousins. And the Booyzen daughter was named **Johanna Catharina** —
after the great-aunt who is the link between them.

## What it adds to the archive
1. **A whole collateral branch**, with thirteen children in the 1848–1878 generation and nine in the next.
2. **George Mountjoy's occupation: SADDLE MAKER** — the first trade recorded for a Mountjoy here.
3. **The parents' marriage: 9 March 1817, Graaff-Reinet** — new, and it places James Mountjoy and Anna
   Catharina at Graaff-Reinet two years before Johanna Catharina's birth there.
4. **The Mountjoys stayed.** James Jonathan died at **Barkly East** in 1937; Hercules Johannes in
   **Wodehouse** in 1941; Maria Sophia van Niekerk lived to **1961**. While the Booyzens went to
   Johannesburg, their Mountjoy cousins remained in the district for another half-century.

## Names crossing between the families
- **Johanna Catharina** — George named a daughter so in **1860**; his sister's grandson's family named one
  so in **1889**. The same name on both branches, from the same source.
- **Willem Hermanus** — a Booysen name, and **George Mountjoy named a son Willem Hermanus in 1872**;
  Hercules Johannes named another in **1915**.
- **Maria Sophia** — Botha, then van Niekerk, then a Mountjoy daughter in 1898. The witness
  *"Maria Sophia Mountjoy"* at the 1889 font is most likely **Maria Sophia Botha (1827–1911)**, the
  child's grandmother, then 62. <span>Inferred</span>

# Pass two — the original records (9 Sep 2026)

The note above ended with a caution: the **arrangement** of the Mountjoy family was FamilySearch's,
not a record's. That caution can now be withdrawn. Going after death notices for Hercules Johannes and
James Jonathan turned up something better — the registers themselves, and a generation further back.

## 1. Hercules Johannes: parentage confirmed from the baptism register

**DRC baptism register, Cape Colony.** Herculas Johannes Mountjoy, **born 16 July 1866, baptised
29 July 1866**; father **George Mountjoy**, mother **Maria Sophia Botha**.
`ark:/61903/1:1:6JYY-3KCN`

His civil **death registration** gives 12 May 1941, Wodehouse, **age 74** (`1:1:QLX3-12G4`). A man born
16 July 1866 is 74 in May 1941 and turns 75 that July. The arithmetic closes exactly.

His **marriage**: Hercules Johannes Montjoy, **aged 22**, to Maria Sophia van Niekerk, aged 20,
**8 October 1888 at Dordrecht, Wodehouse** — indexed four separate times under the spellings
*Montjoy*, *Mondjoy* and *Mountjoy* (`1:1:6ZHB-8T2S`, `6P17-LFGK`, `6P1C-MBD7`, `6GCN-GG1N`).
He married in **October 1888**; the child baptised in the **1889** register this archive read is his
first. The fit is exact.

**So the parentage no longer rests on a user-edited tree.** George Mountjoy → Hercules Johannes is a
church register, and the death registration and marriage both corroborate the birth year.

## 2. No estate file for Hercules Johannes — and that is a finding, not a gap

NAAIRS (Cape Town Archives Repository) searched exhaustively:

| `MOUNTJOY AND WODEHOUSE` | 0 |
| `MOUNTJOY AND BARKLY` | 0 |

There is **no estate file for Hercules Johannes Mountjoy** in KAB under any spelling. He died 12 May
1941; the surrounding estates run 1938, 1939, 1943, 1946, 1948 — the sequence is continuous, so this
is an absence, not a hole in the index. The likeliest reading is an estate too small to require
reporting. His death is therefore documented by the **civil registration**, which does not name
parents — which is precisely why the 1866 baptism above matters.

## 3. James Jonathan = "James John" — the estate file located

**KAB, MOOC 6/9/5047, reference 58379, 1938 — `MOUNTJOY, JAMES JOHN. ESTATE PAPERS.`**

**58380**, is `MOUNTJOY, MARIA LOUISA BERTHA. NEE KUMM. ESTATE PAPERS.` — his wife, who died
3 July 1934. Husband and wife, consecutive file numbers, wound up together.

### Anna Catharina Sleer — the surname resolved
The 1863 death notice reads **Sleer**. The hand is ambiguous between *Sleer* and *Steer*, and this
archive has been burned once already by over-reading a single word. It is settled by convergence from
two independent directions: the family tree carries **Schleher**, FamilySearch's index carries
**Slier**. Three renderings, three sources, all with an **l** and none with a **t**. The letter is an
uncrossed *l*, and the name is **Sleer / Slier**.

### James Montjoy's own death notice — located, not yet read
**KAB MOOC 6/9/21, ref 4393, 1840.** This is the generation above: it would name *his* parents, and
with them the point at which the name enters the Cape. It is **not name-indexed on FamilySearch** —
the "James Montjoy" record there (`1:1:QGPR-RBQ8`) is only his appearance as a *parent* on his
daughter's 1863 notice. The probate collection has 846 films indexed by film number alone, so finding
the 1840 volume means locating the film that covers MOOC 6/9/21. **Open — the single most valuable
next document in this archive.**

## 5. BOOYZEN with a Z in 1851 — and the control that empties it

**DRC baptism register, Graaff-Reinet, Baptism Registers 1851–1856, entry No. 2809:**
**Willem Hermanus**, born **4 May 1851**, baptised **31 October 1851** at **"Achkter Sneewberg"**
(Achter Sneeuwberg); father **Petrus Jacobus Booyzen**, mother **Johanna Catharina Montjoy**.
`ark:/61903/1:1:6RH3-JQX4`, image apid `TH-909-71515-81879-58`.

Read from the **Cape Town Archives scan** at 9× magnification, the father's surname is **not ambiguous**.
It is **Booyzen**, with a clear z carrying a descender.

**And that is where it stops being a discovery.** Three lines below, the same clerk in the same ink
writes the godparents:

> **Willem Hermanus Pieterzen** · Maria Catharina Magdalena Potgieter · **Xaverius Hermanus Pieterzen**

**Pieterzen.** Same letterform, same descender, twice. The Pietersens are not a family with a
name-change story. In Dutch orthography **-zen** is simply how the patronymic ending is written, and
this clerk wrote it that way for everyone who came to his font.

**Conclusion:** the z in *Booyzen* here is the **clerk's spelling convention, not the family's claim on
it**. The z was ordinary Cape-Dutch orthography, lying about in the registers the whole time.

### What this costs, and what it does not
- **Costs:** any reading in which the z was *invented* — in 1905, on the road north, or by one man.
  It also **largely empties the 1879 signature question**: a z-shaped letter in a Dutch hand is not
  evidence of intent. That question is now closed rather than open.
- **Does not cost:** the **1905 death registration**, which turns on *two spellings of one man's name
  on one sheet* (clerk **Booysen**, son **Booyzen**, four lines apart) and not on the shape of a letter.
  Nor the **distribution data** — 646 against 53,764, Gauteng against the Cape.

### A correction to this archive's own earlier reading
Document 24 of `documents-transcribed.md` read this same entry from a **photograph on the family tree**
and recorded the father as **`Boo[i]j[s/z]en`** (ambiguous) and the witnesses as **`Pietersen`**, with
an s — concluding *"I cannot separate them from an image… no scan will establish it."* Both readings
were wrong, and the second was the load-bearing one: **rendering the witnesses with an s hid the
control.** The archive scan settles both. A better scan did establish it — just not in the direction
the archive was hoping for.

# Pass three — the 1840 death notice (9 Sep 2026)

## How it was found
Not name-indexed, and the probate collection's 846 films are catalogued by film number alone. Route:

1. Search collection `2517051` for any **indexed** 1840 death → James Edwards, d. 28 Oct 1840
   (`1:1:QGTD-QLCP`), image ark `3:1:3Q9M-CSQX-MQ2G-6`.
2. Its waypoint is `WV44-SZV:1598082367` = film **007844936**, *"Cape Province. Probate Records
   1840–1843"*, **2,223 images**. Our image sits at index **700**.
3. Reference numbers are pencilled in the top-right corner and **ascend by ~1 per image**:
   image 700 = 4726 · 720 = 4745 · 740 = 4765 · 760 = 4785 · 780 = 4803 · 800 = 4822 · 820 = 4840 ·
   840 = 4861. Target **4393** is therefore *earlier*: 4726 − 4393 = 333, so ≈ image 367.
4. Sampled 330–420: 330 = 4386 · 345 = 4401 · 360 = 4415 · 375 = 4428 · 390 = 4442 · 405 = 4455 ·
   420 = 4470. Bracketed to 330–345, then stepped one by one:
   333 = 4389 · 334 = 4390 · 335 = 4391 · 337 = 4392 · **338 = 4393**.

**Image 338, apid `TH-909-58020-130103-43`.** Method worth reusing for any unindexed MOOC reference.

## The document
**KAB MOOC 6/9/21, reference 4393. Filed 9 July 1840. Signed at Colesberg 25 June 1840.**

| Field | As written |
|---|---|
| 1. Name of the Deceased | **James Montjoy** |
| 2. Birth-place | **Europe** |
| 3. **Names of the Parents** | **Not Known** |
| 4. Age | **About 45 years** |
| 5. Condition in life | **Saddler** |
| 6. Married or unmarried | **Married to Catharina Sl[i/e]er, widow of William Pietersen** |
| 7. Day of decease | **September 1827** |
| 8. At what House | **In the village of Cradock** |
| 9. Children | 1. James, a minor · 2. George, do. · 3. Johanna, do. · 4. Jacoba, do. |
| 10. Property | **A house and erf at Cradock, of the value of £90** |
| Signed | a Montjoy — the initial is not legible with confidence |

## What it gives
1. **"Not Known."** The document sought to break the wall *is* the wall. Written **thirteen years after
   the death**, by someone who knew his trade, his house, its value, the village and the name of his
   wife's first husband — and could not name his parents. The line stops here, and it stops for a
   reason internal to the family. Exact parallel to the 1915 Kolbe notice ("Father: Kolbe, thinks James
   was christian name. Mother: Unknown") — same box, same printed form, seventy-five years apart.
2. **"Europe."** Not a country. This is now the only statement of origin in hand.
3. **Saddler.** His son George Mountjoy is a **saddle maker** at Cradock at his 1848 marriage. George was
   **four** when his father died. The trade survived the father. The note in pass one calling George's
   trade "the first recorded for a Mountjoy" is wrong — it is the second.
4. **The Pietersen marriage, documented.** Pass two inferred, from a godparent at the 1851 font, that
   Johanna Catharina's half-brothers were Pieterzens. Line 6 names the first marriage outright.
5. **Property.** A house and erf at Cradock, £90. He was a town tradesman with freehold, not a bywoner.

## Corrections this makes
- **The 1827/1840 contradiction was mine, not the tree's.** He died September 1827; the file is dated
  by its **filing** year, 1840. The tree's 1827 is right. Recorded on the site as such.
- **Age "about 45" in Sept 1827 → born c. 1782.** The tree has 1786. Close, and the notice says "about".
- **Thomas Jacobus (b. 1818) is absent from the children.** Independent support for his death in 1827.
- **James Mountjoy is NOT "1826–1826".** He is listed **alive, a minor**, in September 1827. The tree's
  infant-death date is wrong.
- The four children fix the family at Sept 1827: Johanna Catharina 8, Jacoba 7, George 4, James 1.

## Where the line goes now
Nothing further is recoverable inside South Africa. Above James Montjoy the search must move **outside
the Cape**: English, Irish or Continental records for a **saddler named Montjoy/Mountjoy, born c. 1782,
in the Cape before 1818** (his eldest child was born at Graaff-Reinet that year).
