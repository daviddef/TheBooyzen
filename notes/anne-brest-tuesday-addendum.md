## The gap, and it is the one that matters to the Kolbes

**The invoice was raised on 16 September. David's long reply adding more work was sent the same
day, after it.** That reply added Groups A, B and C — nineteen further items across three
families — and said: *"If it changes the invoice, send me a revised one and I will pay that
instead."*

**No revised invoice has come.** Anne's latest email says only *"I have made all the arrangements
for your files for Tuesday coming. And your free file"* — which reads like the seven on the
invoice and nothing else.

So the additional list may not be in scope for Tuesday. **Including this:**

> **A1. TAB MHG 40293 — KOLBE, Frederick Fortunatus Downing, 1918. Death notice only.**

**This is now the single most valuable item on the whole Pretoria list**, and it was worth less
when David wrote it than it is today. "Downing" is carried here as a *forename* — it was Margaret
Downing's maiden name, and she married the 1820 Settler George Augustus Kolbe in London in 1819.
**If that death notice names his father, it may connect the Transvaal Kolbes to the Settler
directly.** Everything this archive holds on that descent currently reaches it by inference and by
nothing else. The Pietermaritzburg estate files were wanted for exactly the same reason and are
still unordered.

## Standing, for when the photographs come back

- **MHG 52653 is expected to fail.** David has twice told her not to hunt it, and she has twice
  said noted. If it is not there, **the useful outcome is the correct reference from the paper
  index, not the file** — and she is the one person who can look at the paper index.
- **Write up the misses at the same length as the finds.** A "not there" from inside the building,
  with a reason, is a controlled negative and this archive publishes those.
- **The death notice is the page**: father, mother, birthplace, occupation, surviving spouse, and
  the list of children.
