# Decoding a name: George Downing Mountjoy Booyzen

David Defranceski's great-grandfather was christened **George Downing Mountjoy Booyzen** (1901–1947).
On an Afrikaans surname that reads like an affectation. It is the opposite. **Every one of those three
forenames is a maternal surname**, carried forward in the old Cape way of keeping the women's families
visible in the men's names.

| Name | Comes from | Who they were |
|---|---|---|
| **George** | **George Augustus Kolbe** (1802–1844) | his great-great-grandfather; recorded in the tree under an English flag |
| **Downing** | **Margaret Georgina Elizabeth Downing** (1803–1867) | George Augustus Kolbe's wife — his great-great-great-grandmother |
| **Mountjoy** | **Johanna Catharina Mountjoy** (1819–1863) | his great-grandmother, who married Petrus Jacobus Booysen at Cradock in 1838 |
| *Booyzen* | **Willem Hermanus Booyzen** (1851–1905) | his father — who is recorded as having changed it from **Booysen** |

So the name is a compressed pedigree: **Kolbe · Downing · Mountjoy · Booysen**. Three British families
and one Cape Dutch one, in a single christening.

The reinforcement is visible elsewhere in the same generation: **George Augustus Barry** (1850–1933) and
**John Augustus Barry** I and II carry the Kolbe forename "Augustus" too, and **David Kolbe Booyzen**
(1896–1980) carries the Kolbe surname outright. **Frederik Fortsenatus Booÿsen** (b. 1899) echoes
**Frederick Fortunatus Barry** (1863–1952).

This is the archive's clearest single demonstration that **the women's families did not vanish into the
Booyzen name — they were carried inside it.**
