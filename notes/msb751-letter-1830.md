# MSB 751 — the letter of 23 August 1830, read 20 September 2026

Sent by **Melanie Geustyn, Senior Librarian**, National Library of South Africa,
Cape Town Campus, on **18 September 2026**, three days after the enquiry went in.
Two PDFs: the manuscript letter (4 pages) and a two-page **DSAB** biography by
**Dr Karel Schoeman** with a list of Bronne. Read from the manuscript.

---

## What it is

Four sides in one hand, headed **"London August 23. 1830"**, opening
**"Dear George,"** and signed **"Your affectionate father, J G Kolbe."**

**It is the first document in this archive written by Johan Gottlieb Kolbe
himself.** Everything else — the rate books, the Gazette, the census, the burial
register — is somebody writing *about* him.

---

## The four questions that were asked, answered

**1. A copy of the letter.** Supplied.

**2. Does it carry an address?** **NO.** The heading is "London" and nothing
finer. *It does not settle Brunswick Square against Conduit Street.* That was
the question most hoped for and the answer is a clean negative.

**3. What are the other pages?** The four pages are the letter. There is no
second item.

**4. Is "LMS missionary" from the papers or from the donor?** **From the
papers, in the father's own words.** The letter congratulates George on having
been "fully accepted by the [L.M.S.] as one of their Missionaries" and hopes for
"a more favourable account of the poor Bushmen". **It is independent of John
Montgomery's memoir**, which was written down forty-five years after the fact.

**And the Background File** holds nothing further on the donation or on any
other deposit by Dr Griffiths — so the whereabouts of the original is not
recorded and cannot be pursued from that end.

## What the letter says that nobody knew

### The LMS, and Dr Philip

- "About a month ago I wrote to you by **Dr Philip**" — John Philip, the LMS
  superintendent at the Cape, carried an earlier letter from father to son.
- "**Mr Miles** had called at our house & informed me that you had been fully
  accepted by the [L.M.S.] as one of their Missionaries."
- "**Dr Philip** was exceedingly glad to hear of your being accepted by the
  L.M.S. as you may well imagine; he has promised to write to you a letter by
  the first opportunity."
- The prayer is that God will "bless your ministry amongst **the poor Bushman &
  other heathens**".
- Charlotte sends an **Evangelical Magazine** carrying "the account of the death
  of the **Rev. W. Orme, late Secretary to the L.M.S.**"

### Henry, dying

The longest passage in the letter is about a brother.

- "**Henry** had wrote to you himself & left his letter at the Mission house to
  be forwarded to you with the first opportunity."
- "he keeps up pretty well at present, but what effect the winter may have upon
  him if it should set in very cold, as it did last winter, is perhaps not
  doubtful, since it must prevent him from taking exercise by walking, & which
  is a great means of keeping down **the Dropsy**, of which he bears still the
  Symptoms."
- "I trust also that you will join your prayers, that he may vouchsafe to
  prepare him to a fit state of meeting his God… **he appears at present to be
  resigned to the Catastrophe**."

**This is John Henry Kolbe, baptised 29 July 1800, who died 13 April 1832 and
was buried in the German Lutheran register at TNA RG 4/4631.** This archive
found his baptism, his 1819 Old Bailey deposition, his 1819 marriage to Matilda
Ford, his 1824 bankruptcy and his burial. **Now it has his illness and his
father's grief, twenty months before the end.**

### "your Sisters & brother" — and the eight sons

The closing sends "kind love from **your Sisters & brother**".

**Sisters plural. Brother singular.** In August 1830, of the eight sons of the
1844 obituary, **exactly two were living**: George Augustus, and Henry, who was
dying. Henry died on 13 April 1832.

**From that date George Augustus was "the last remaining of eight sons" —
twelve years before the Grahamstown Journal said so.** The obituary is
corroborated and the date it became true is now known.

### Elizabeth, who married John Milne

- "**Milne** with **Elizabeth** & family had transferred their residence to
  **Edinburgh**; Milne has just come to London on the way of his business, he
  says that **Betsey** likes the situation very well, & they think that they
  will settle there rather than in London; Milne & Betsey desire their best to
  you."

On 16 September this archive found, in the Westminster marriage register,
**Elizabeth Kolbe married John Milne in 1817 at St Clement Danes**. That was a
line in an index. **This is her father calling her Betsey and saying where she
moved to.**

### Charlotte

"**Charlotte** sends you an Evangelical Magazine."

On 17 September this archive wrote that Marianne, Charlotte and Amelia of the
1841 Conduit Street household "match no baptism in the family's own register
run", and that their place in the family was **an inference from living in the
house**. **Charlotte's is an inference no longer.** Her father names her sending
a magazine to her brother.

### What a father sends a missionary son

"I do herewith send you **a Suit of Black clothes**, hope they may be wide
enough for you, as I understand that you are stout as long" — clerical black for
a newly accepted missionary — with **a sash and waistcoat**, and **a set of
Newspapers of last year's**, some of which Henry, who read them every week after
his father had done with them, appears to have lost.

### The world, in August 1830

- "on the 26 June last we lost by death our King **George 4th** and have now a
  **William the 4th, formerly Duke of Clarence**, who has become very popular;
  he appears to be a King after the people's heart"
- "in France a **New Revolution** has taken place, where the King **Charles
  10th** & his Ministers had become the Revolutionists, who by some wicked
  Ordinances attempted to crush the liberty of the Subject & to make himself
  **an absolute King**"

The July Revolution was barely three weeks old when he wrote.

### And a tall story he wants checked

He has read in a magazine called **The Mirror** of a **Scottish colonist**,
"either **Williams or Williamson**", settled close to the Caffres, whose
settlement was raided by baboons; a servant shot one, and the troop is said to
have come back for revenge, the dead animal being a son of their chief. London
"looks upon the story as a fictitious romance", but the magazine affirms it as
true and says the man has quitted the Colony and returned to Scotland.

**"Should there be any thing like truth in it, I should think that your Society
would have heard some thing about it, & therefore I should be glad to hear from
you, whether that report may be founded in truth or falsehood."**

A tailor in London asking his missionary son to fact-check a magazine.
